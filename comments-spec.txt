
2025-10-18 Comments on spec 2.1 (and what a shame that this is the first full draft I get to see.)

1. I see a lot of bullet points. Normally we number everything to facilitate unambiguous 
referencing in discussion.

2. ATs are okay so far as they go, but possibly don't tell enough. This is a "plan" meaning
we'd also like to know who is doing these activities, when, and when maybe more crisply how
anyone would recognize they are concluded successfully. How many of these tests must pass
in order for us to conclude the project is a success? I guess all of them? We should have
said a grand aggregate determination.

3. We refer three times to installation but explain nothing about what steps the team might
take to prepare a package to be installed or how we intend to do it.  This is one of the 
kinds of cross checks we perform - checking that the terms used in one section are reflected
the same way in others, or if they are missing altogether.

4. We don't see traceability from cost section to design in order to check whether we agree
on the complexity estimates. The figures given in #8 are just given, not supported. 

5. The cost estimate ONLY uses FPA, which is fine so far as it goes, but remember FPA does
not account for ALL time, just time in the construction of a given design. You have other
kinds of time that will be spent on this. How much is that? We should account for it too.

6. The timeline is a reasonable starting point but doesn't tell me any commitment on what
you will demo on the lab days 10-23, 10-30, 11-6. We'd like to see sequence of increasing
levels of functionality - "This week we now have...." - en route to the CDR.  Potentially
you would have wanted to express this in a Gantt chart in order to track dependencies as
well.  Remember, the predicted time is not equally divisible by team members. Some tasks
must precede others and so on. It is not like just clocking hours at a minimum wage job.

7. This seems awfully light on wireframe and piloting illustrations. How do you know you
are on track to offering a good UX for this task?

8. I see no mentino of design alternatives. Did you ultimately decide that the React env
was best to use after all? Apparently so. Why?  At a minimum you'd want to preserve this
sort of material in anticipation of filling in content on the final report (which again 
I recommend you check out now so there is not a fire drill later in recreating design info.)

9. We talk in a few places about dealing with this at "large scale" which is good, that is
our intention, but we nowhere say what that means nor how we will test it. The same is true
of visualiztion. We promise this in dozens of places, but again don't say that much about
how we will achieve it or control it. Let's be clear: I will interpret absence of further 
detail about this as a promise that it will meet MY sense of large scale and visualization
capability satisfactorily. (This will entail potentially tens of thosuands of nodes, with 
potentially elaborate state charts controlling each in the simulation. It will entail the
ability to manipulate the viewport to render different perspectives and levels of detail.)
This will need be smooth to set up, run and visualize. A toy that barely operates on just
a dozen nodes is a toy and serve as a fail. The risk reduction steps during piloting stage
we just completed might reasonably have evaluated these performance and vis needs too.


Overall it comes across as going in the right direction. I'd like to have polished a few
more details and filled in small gaps before moving ahead (per my kvetching above) but it
is close enough to green light in the "low A" range (recognizing that we leave open risks
unaddressed w.r.t. scale and vis properties.) Looking forward to a big win on this project!
